§ 01

About

Materials synthesis and electrode fabrication for electrochemical energy-storage devices, from miniature on-chip cells to next-generation grid chemistries.

Dr. Habtom Desta Asfaw is a researcher at the Department of Chemistry — Ångström Laboratory, Uppsala University, where he specialises in materials synthesis and electrode fabrication for electrochemical energy-storage devices, including batteries and supercapacitors.

His work bridges fundamental electrochemistry and applied materials engineering — designing electrode architectures, probing electrode–electrolyte interfaces, and developing next-generation chemistries such as dual-ion and solid-state batteries.

Originally from Ethiopia, Dr. Asfaw obtained his PhD at Uppsala University in 2017, held postdoctoral positions at Imperial College London and Empa (Switzerland), and returned to Uppsala as a researcher in 2019.

§ 02

Research

Beyond intrinsic material properties, the performance of an electrochemical device is governed by electrode architecture and the stability of electrode–electrolyte interfaces.

"Beyond intrinsic material properties, the performance of an electrochemical system is governed by the electrode's architecture and the stability of its interfaces with the electrolyte."

Dual-ion batteries

Unlike conventional lithium-ion batteries, dual-ion batteries store charge by intercalating both anions and cations into their respective electrodes. The group has developed graphite-based potassium and lithium dual-ion cells, explored highly concentrated electrolytes (HCEs) and ionogel electrolytes, and probed the cathode–electrolyte interphase using XPS and operando Raman spectro-electrochemistry.

Carbon anode materials

Carbon dominates commercial Li-ion anodes (graphite) and is the leading candidate for Na-ion anodes (hard carbon). Dr. Asfaw has worked across both extremes — from highly ordered graphite to disordered hard carbons synthesised from polyphenols, lignosulfonates, biomass pyrolysis bio-oil, and catalytically graphitised biochar.

Biomass-derived hard carbons

Most commercial hard carbons originate from petroleum-based precursors. The group has demonstrated routes from lignin, cellulose, polyphenols, and even biomass pyrolysis bio-oil — substantially lowering the embodied carbon footprint of sodium-ion anodes. Recent work explores induction-heated carbonisation and catalytic graphitisation of biochar.

3D microbatteries

Dr. Asfaw's PhD work pioneered the use of emulsion-templated polyHIPE-derived carbon foams as 3D scaffolds for Li-ion microbatteries. The interconnected porous architecture decouples capacity from footprint area — critical for powering MEMS devices, sensors, and on-chip electronics.

Structural supercapacitors

At Imperial College London, Dr. Asfaw helped develop carbon-aerogel-reinforced carbon-fibre fabrics — composites that simultaneously bear mechanical load and store electrical energy at the electric double layer. Applied to aerospace or automotive structures, such materials enable "massless" energy storage.

Alkali-ion chemistries

Each alkali-ion chemistry imposes different demands on host materials. Sodium's larger ion does not intercalate effectively into graphite — driving the need for hard carbon. Potassium can use graphite (KC₈) but at the cost of significant volume change. These trade-offs shape much of the group's materials design work.

Electrode–electrolyte interphases (SEI & CEI)

In dual-ion batteries the cathode-electrolyte interphase (CEI) on graphite is just as critical as the well-studied SEI on the anode. Probing how these layers form, what they contain, and how they evolve under cycling is a central theme of recent group work.

Operando Raman spectro-electrochemistry

A custom in-situ Raman cell with an optical window allows the group to track the structural evolution of carbon electrodes during electrochemical cycling, with the D and G bands serving as fingerprints for staging, intercalation, and SEI growth.
